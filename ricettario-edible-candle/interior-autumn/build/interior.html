Introduction

Welcome to the
Autumn Table

When the leaves turn copper and the evenings draw in, there is no better reason to gather friends around the table than a candle you can eat.

A butter candle looks like a beautiful pillar candle, but it is made entirely of flavored butter, shaped in a simple silicone mold and finished with a short length of plain cotton twine. Set it on a small plate in the middle of the table and it becomes the conversation piece of the evening, until someone reaches for the bread.

The wick is purely decorative: it stays unlit and comes out before serving. Let the candle soften for about ten minutes, then spread it over warm toast, waffles or brioche.

Every recipe in this book takes about ten minutes of hands-on time, a few everyday ingredients and a couple of hours in the fridge. Start with the base method on page 8, then let the season guide your flavors.

01 Savory Butter Candles

Roasted Garlic & Rosemary Butter Candle

Sweet roasted garlic and fragrant rosemary turn a simple butter spread into a centerpiece for warm, crusty bread.

Makes1 candle
Servesabout 8
Prep10 min
Roast35–45 min
Chill2 hr

Ingredients

  • 1 small head garlic
  • 1 tsp olive oil
  • ½ cup (113 g) unsalted butter, softened
  • 1 tsp finely chopped fresh rosemary
  • ¼ tsp fine sea salt
  • ⅛ tsp freshly ground black pepper
  • Toasted baguette slices, for serving
You’ll also need a small food-grade silicone spiral mold (about ½ cup capacity) and a short piece of plain, undyed cotton kitchen twine for the decorative wick.

Method

  1. 1

    Roast the garlic. Heat the oven to 400°F (200°C). Slice the top off the garlic head to expose the cloves. Drizzle with olive oil, wrap in foil, and roast for 35–45 minutes, until soft and golden. Let cool.

  2. 2

    Mix the butter. Squeeze 4 roasted cloves into a bowl and mash until smooth. Stir in the softened butter, rosemary, salt, and pepper.

  3. 3

    Fill the mold. Press the butter into the mold with the back of a spoon to push it into the grooves. Smooth the exposed surface and refrigerate for about 2 hours, until firm.

  4. 4

    Add the wick. Unmold onto a small ceramic plate. Use a skewer to make a shallow hole in the center of the top, then insert the cotton twine so a short length stands upright.

  5. 5

    Serve. Let the butter soften for about 10 minutes. Keep the wick unlit and remove it before serving. Spread the butter over warm baguette slices.

02 Savory Butter Candles

Smoked Paprika & Sea Salt Butter Candle

Sweet smoked paprika gives this butter its copper color and a woodsmoke aroma. A pinch of flaky sea salt adds crunch to each bite of grilled sourdough.

Makes1 candle
Servesabout 8
Prep10 min
Chill2 hr

Ingredients

  • ½ cup (113 g) unsalted butter, softened
  • 1½ tsp sweet smoked paprika
  • ½ tsp finely grated lemon zest
  • ¼ tsp fine sea salt
  • 1 pinch flaky sea salt, to finish
  • Grilled sourdough slices, for serving
You’ll also need a small cylindrical food-grade silicone mold (about ½ cup capacity) and a short piece of plain, undyed cotton kitchen twine for the decorative wick.

Method

  1. 1

    Season the butter. Mix the softened butter with the paprika, lemon zest, and fine sea salt until the color is uniform.

  2. 2

    Fill the mold. Press the butter into the mold a spoonful at a time to minimize air pockets. Level the exposed surface and refrigerate for about 2 hours, until firm.

  3. 3

    Add the wick. Unmold the butter onto a small ceramic plate. Make a shallow hole in the center of the top with a skewer and insert the twine so a short length stands upright. Sprinkle the flaky salt around it.

  4. 4

    Serve. Let the butter soften for about 10 minutes. Keep the wick unlit and remove it before serving. Spread the butter on warm grilled sourdough.

03 Sweet Butter Candles

Fig & Honey Butter Candle

Fig preserves leave little flecks of fruit in this honey-sweetened butter. Serve it with warm brioche, fresh figs, and toasted walnuts for crunch.

Makes1 candle
Servesabout 8
Prep10 min
Chill2 hr

Ingredients

  • ½ cup (113 g) unsalted butter, softened
  • 1 tbsp thick fig preserves
  • 1 tsp honey
  • 1 pinch fine sea salt
  • Toasted brioche, quartered fresh figs, and toasted walnuts, to serve
You’ll also need a small fluted food-grade silicone mold (about ⅔ cup capacity) and a short piece of plain, undyed cotton kitchen twine for the decorative wick.

Method

  1. 1

    Mix the butter. Stir the softened butter, honey, and salt until smooth. Fold in the fig preserves, leaving a few streaks and flecks of fruit.

  2. 2

    Fill the mold. Press the butter into the fluted mold with the back of a spoon to fill the grooves. Smooth the exposed surface and refrigerate for about 2 hours, until firm.

  3. 3

    Add the wick. Unmold onto a small ceramic plate. Make a shallow hole in the center of the top with a skewer and insert the twine so a short length stands upright.

  4. 4

    Serve. Let the butter soften for about 10 minutes. Keep the wick unlit and remove it before serving. Spread the butter on warm brioche and serve with the fresh figs and toasted walnuts.

04 Sweet Butter Candles

Maple & Cinnamon Butter Candle

Maple syrup and cinnamon make a soft, lightly sweet butter that melts into the pockets of a hot waffle.

Makes1 candle
Servesabout 8
Prep10 min
Chill2 hr

Ingredients

  • ½ cup (113 g) unsalted butter, softened
  • 1 tbsp pure maple syrup
  • ½ tsp ground cinnamon
  • 1 pinch fine sea salt
  • Warm waffles and extra maple syrup, to serve
You’ll also need a small round food-grade silicone mold (about ⅔ cup capacity) and a short piece of plain, undyed cotton kitchen twine for the decorative wick.

Method

  1. 1

    Mix the butter. Stir the softened butter with the cinnamon and salt until evenly speckled. Add the maple syrup a teaspoon at a time, stirring well after each addition.

  2. 2

    Shape and chill. Press the butter into the mold with the back of a spoon. Smooth the surface and refrigerate for about 2 hours, until firm enough to unmold.

  3. 3

    Add the wick. Turn the butter out onto a small saucer. Make a shallow hole in the center with a skewer and insert the twine so a short length stands upright.

  4. 4

    Serve. Let the butter soften for about 10 minutes. Keep the wick unlit and remove it before serving. Spoon the butter over hot waffles and finish with a drizzle of maple syrup.

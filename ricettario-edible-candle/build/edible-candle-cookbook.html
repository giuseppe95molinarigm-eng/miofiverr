Introduction

Welcome to the
Edible Candle Table

There is a moment at every good dinner when the conversation slows and everyone reaches for the bread. This book is about that moment, and about making it glow.

An edible candle is exactly what it sounds like: a candle shaped from butter, tallow, cocoa butter or another food-grade fat, set around a food-safe wick. Lit at the table, it melts into a warm, fragrant pool that becomes a dip. What starts as decoration ends as the most talked-about course of the evening.

Edible candles are surprisingly simple. Most need fewer than six ingredients, a paper cup for a mold and a few hours in the fridge. Once you know the base method on page 6, you can flavor them for any season: herby and garlicky for a spring supper, smoky and spiced for a winter roast, sweet and vanilla-scented for dessert.

Every recipe in these pages has been tested for flavor, for how cleanly it burns and for how long it lasts on the table. You will find burn times, make-ahead notes and pairing ideas alongside each one.

Light one tonight. You’ll see.
Eleanor

Before You Light

Tools & Safety

Food-safe wicks

Choose 100% unbleached cotton wicks with no wax coating, dyes or metal core. Prime with olive oil before use.

Simple molds

Paper cups, short glasses, ramekins or silicone molds. Line paper and glass with parchment for a clean release.

A steady base

Always serve on a rimmed, heat-resistant plate or board that can catch the melting pool.

Long matches

A long match or lighter keeps hands clear of the soft surface and lets you relight easily.

The five golden rules

  1. Never leave it alone. A lit candle is supervised from first spark to last dip.
  2. Trim to ¼ in. A short wick gives a small, clean flame that melts rather than burns.
  3. Blow out before you dip. Or dip from the outer edge, away from the flame.
  4. Discard the wick. It is never eaten. Pull it out with tongs once cool.
  5. Mind allergies. Label nut and dairy candles when guests are sharing.

The Base Method
in Four Steps

Every candle in this book follows the same four steps. Learn them here and each recipe becomes a simple change of flavor.

01

Soften & flavor

Beat room-temperature fat until smooth, then fold in herbs, spices or sweeteners.

02

Set the wick

Center a primed wick in the mold and hold it upright with a clothespin.

03

Pack & chill

Fill in layers, tap out air pockets and chill until completely firm.

04

Unmold & light

Release onto a plate, trim the wick and light 10 minutes before serving.

Savory Butter Candles

Garlic & Herb Butter Candle

The candle that started the trend. Light it ten minutes before guests sit down and a warm pool of garlicky butter collects at the top, ready for torn bread. By the end of the meal, the candle has quietly become the appetizer.

Serves6–8
Prep15 min
Set3 hrs
Burn45–60 min

Ingredients

  • 1 cup (2 sticks / 225 g) salted butter, softened
  • 3 garlic cloves, finely grated
  • 2 tbsp fresh parsley, minced
  • 1 tbsp fresh chives, minced
  • 1 tsp fresh thyme leaves
  • ½ tsp flaky sea salt
  • ¼ tsp cracked black pepper
  • To assemble
  • 1 food-safe cotton wick, 6 in
  • 1 tsp olive oil, to prime the wick
  • 1 tall 8 oz paper cup, as mold

Method

  1. 1

    Prime the wick by rubbing it with olive oil until fully coated. Set aside on a paper towel.

  2. 2

    In a bowl, beat the softened butter with a spatula until smooth and glossy. Fold in garlic, herbs, salt and pepper until evenly speckled.

  3. 3

    Line the mold with parchment. Hold the wick upright in the center with a clothespin and pack in the butter in three layers, tapping out any air pockets.

  4. 4

    Refrigerate for at least 3 hours, or freeze for 1 hour, until completely firm.

  5. 5

    Unmold onto a rimmed plate, trim the wick to ¼ in and finish with flaky salt. Light 10 minutes before serving and dip warm bread into the melted pool.

Chef’s note Make the candle up to 5 days ahead and keep it wrapped in the fridge. Always blow it out before scooping the last of the butter.

Hot Honey Beef Tallow Candle

Tallow burns slower and cleaner than butter, so this candle lasts the whole dinner. As it melts, swirl in a ribbon of chili-spiked honey for a sweet-heat dip that disappears faster than the bread basket can be refilled.

Serves6
Prep20 min
Set2 hrs
Burn90+ min

Ingredients

  • 1 cup (200 g) rendered beef tallow
  • 1 sprig fresh rosemary
  • 1 garlic clove, smashed
  • ½ tsp smoked sea salt
  • Hot honey
  • ⅓ cup (110 g) runny honey
  • 1–2 tsp red chili flakes
  • 1 tsp apple cider vinegar
  • To assemble
  • 1 food-safe cotton wick with metal tab
  • 1 oven-safe 8 oz ramekin

Method

  1. 1

    Gently melt the tallow with the rosemary and garlic over low heat for 10 minutes to infuse. Do not let it smoke. Strain and stir in the smoked salt.

  2. 2

    Fix the wick tab to the center of the ramekin with a drop of the warm tallow. Let it set for one minute, then pour in the infused tallow.

  3. 3

    Leave at room temperature for 2 hours, or chill for 45 minutes, until opaque and firm.

  4. 4

    Meanwhile, warm honey, chili flakes and vinegar for 3 minutes. Cool slightly.

  5. 5

    Light the candle at the table. Once a melted pool forms, drizzle in the hot honey and dip toasted sourdough straight into the ramekin.

Swap it No tallow? Use ghee for a nutty, vegetarian version with the same long, steady burn.

Sweet Dipping Candles

Vanilla Bean Cocoa Butter Candle

A dessert you light instead of plate. Pure cocoa butter melts into a silky, white-chocolate-scented dip for strawberries, shortbread and salted pretzels. Flecked with real vanilla, it looks as good on the table as it tastes.

Serves4–6
Prep15 min
Set2 hrs
Burn40–50 min

Ingredients

  • ¾ cup (150 g) food-grade cocoa butter wafers
  • 2 tbsp refined coconut oil
  • 1 vanilla bean, seeds scraped
  • Pinch of flaky sea salt
  • For dipping
  • 1 lb (450 g) fresh strawberries
  • Shortbread, pretzels or biscotti
  • To assemble
  • 1 food-safe cotton wick, 6 in
  • 1 short 6 oz glass or silicone mold

Method

  1. 1

    Melt the cocoa butter and coconut oil in a heatproof bowl set over barely simmering water, stirring until clear and fluid.

  2. 2

    Remove from the heat and whisk in the vanilla seeds and salt. Let cool for 8–10 minutes, stirring often, until slightly thickened so the seeds stay suspended.

  3. 3

    Center the wick in the mold and pour in the mixture. Tap gently to settle.

  4. 4

    Chill for 2 hours until completely firm, then unmold onto a serving board.

  5. 5

    Light the candle and arrange the fruit and biscuits around it. Dip once a warm pool has formed, about 8 minutes.

Keep it neat Trim the wick to ¼ in before every lighting. A short flame melts the cocoa butter gently without scorching the vanilla.

Banana & Almond Trick Candles

The party trick every kid (and grown-up) asks for again. A banana “candle” with a sliver of almond as its wick really does burn for a minute or two, thanks to the natural oil in the nut. Blow it out, then eat the whole thing.

Serves4
Prep10 min
SetNone
Burn1–2 min

Ingredients

  • 2 firm, just-ripe bananas
  • 4 raw almond slivers (not toasted)
  • ½ lemon, juiced
  • Chocolate drizzle
  • 3 oz (85 g) dark chocolate, chopped
  • 3 tbsp heavy cream
  • Pinch of cinnamon

Method

  1. 1

    Peel the bananas and cut each into two pieces with flat ends, 3–4 in tall. Brush with lemon juice to keep them bright.

  2. 2

    Stand the pieces upright on a serving plate. With a skewer, make a small hole in the top of each and press in an almond sliver, leaving ¼ in exposed.

  3. 3

    Warm the cream until steaming, pour over the chocolate and cinnamon, wait 1 minute and stir until glossy.

  4. 4

    Dim the lights, light the almond “wicks” with a long match and let them flicker. Blow out, drizzle with chocolate and serve immediately.

Allergy note Contains tree nuts. For a nut-free version, use a thin strip of coconut flesh, which burns briefly in the same way.
